# Property and financial due-diligence pattern

Use this when a mixed financial corpus is being reviewed for a rental, credit, guarantor, or ownership decision.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Start from the corpus map, taxpayer/user context, validation manifest, and preferred transcripts.
2. Reconcile expected active-source and page counts; keep user-designated redundant/archive material outside completeness totals.
3. For title and encumbrance conclusions, use the current certificate of tradition/registry record as the ownership authority. A cadastral certificate supports physical/economic data but does not establish title and may lag ownership changes.
4. Treat tax returns, annual income, year-end balances, and old payslips as historical solvency evidence, not proof of current employment or liquidity. Ask for or identify fresh employment and balance certificates when current capacity matters.
5. Minimize disclosure: recommend the smallest sufficient package first; hold full tax returns, complete statements, and unrelated records in reserve.

## Reading registry annotations

- Never conclude that an encumbrance is active merely because its creation appears in the history.
- Trace every relevant annotation forward: creation → cancellation reference → later ownership transfer → any later reconstitution.
- Distinguish current limitations from canceled mortgages, embargoes, family-home affectations, and other historical entries.
- State fractional ownership precisely. Do not silently treat a registered quota as sole ownership or imply that co-owners' shares secure the person's obligations.
- Check issuance date against any recency threshold using the live date.
- Visually inspect the decisive original page when the conclusion depends on exact annotation numbers, cancellation language, ownership percentages, dates, or identifiers—even when a validated transcript exists.

## Decision output

Separate:

1. **Document fact:** what the record expressly shows.
2. **Legal implication:** what an authoritative statute or professional interpretation supports.
3. **Counterparty discretion:** requirements a private landlord/lender may still accept or reject.
4. **Practical package:** primary structure, fallback structures, and least-sensitive supporting documents.

For residential rentals, distinguish a property certificate used only as evidence of solvency from an actual mortgage, cash deposit, or real security. Verify current statutory restrictions before recommending any security. Explain contract roles accurately: a person cannot add independent protection by acting as their own guarantor; use a separate guarantor or jointly liable co-tenants where legally appropriate.

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Active source/page totals reconciled
- [ ] Decisive registry page visually checked
- [ ] Creation and cancellation annotations linked correctly
- [ ] Current owner and exact percentage stated
- [ ] Registry/cadastral inconsistencies disclosed
- [ ] Historical and current financial evidence separated
- [ ] Recency calculated from the live date
- [ ] Legal claims grounded in authoritative sources
- [ ] Recommended disclosure package is privacy-minimized
- [ ] Private counterparty discretion is not misstated as a statutory rule
